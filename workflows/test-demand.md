# Workflow: Test Demand and Initial Acquisition

**Trigger:** a material uncertainty about the customer, offer or route to initial users; or a user request for customer research, a smoke test or early acquisition testing. This is a shared workflow, not a new stage or exclusive Agent 1 execution lane.

## Specialist ownership

| Work | Owner and required method |
|---|---|
| What must be learned, existing evidence, investment decision and verdict | Agent 1, using the recorded assessment and evidence standards |
| Offer, positioning, channel, acquisition copy, distribution and measurement rules | Agent 3, using its stage/market diagnostic and marketing panel |
| Visual design, interaction, page/form/demo implementation, instrumentation and protection | Agent 2, using all seven build/protection lenses and `workflows/scope-build.md` |
| Interpreting actual results | Agent 1 with Agent 3's acquisition evidence and Agent 2's technical verification |

Load each applicable definition before doing its work. Record real decisions or reasoned clearances and resolve consequential conflicts. The same assistant can apply these roles; this does not require separate chats or subagent spawning. Use a compact shared decision record in the existing output to avoid duplicate briefs. On follow-ups, carry forward valid decisions and review only what changes.

A typical sequence is: Agent 1 frames the question; Agent 3 specifies the offer, audience, action and measurement; Agent 2 designs, builds and checks the experience; Agent 3 runs authorized distribution; Agent 1 and Agent 3 interpret the result. Dependencies can iterate. Do not require a full PASS, finished product or ceremonial approval at each role boundary for an already authorized bounded experiment. The project may remain at validation throughout. Full product investment remains a separate decision supported by evidence.

## 1. Resume the actual question

Read the law, index, relevant project memory and existing assessment. Preserve the selected venture/livelihood mode, constraints and authorizations. Identify the claim that needs evidence and what is already known. Do not rerun validation merely to design a test, or demand a smoke test where existing evidence answers the question. A historical KILL for missing evidence may have a testable flip condition; testing it does not itself change the verdict.

Separate three questions: do these people have the problem, will they choose this offer, and can this founder acquire and serve them sustainably? Use the market/adoption comparison from Agent 1. Neither a broad population nor competitor presence answers all three.

## 2. Capture customer evidence

Use a compact evidence table, with one row per distinct observation:

| Source and date | Customer and context | Words or behavior observed | Current solution and awareness | Implication and uncertainty |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Link or interview identifier | Relevant segment and recent trigger | Mark exact quote, paraphrase or observation | Include manual work, no action, first adoption, addition or replacement | State what it supports and what it does not |

Seek specific recent incidents: what happened, how often, what they did, what they tried or paid for, and what stopped them. Ask about unaided awareness before showing competitors if awareness is the research question. Do not require people to try every alternative. Pain ratings and hypothetical purchase answers can guide interviews, but cannot substitute for observed behavior or willingness to pay. Record counterexamples as well as enthusiastic responses. Label AI-generated ideas and inferred pain points as hypotheses until checked against sources.

## 3. Define the offer and entry approach

State the customer, trigger, useful outcome, product availability, price or pricing hypothesis, and requested next action in plain language. Choose replication, differentiation, innovation or a named combination. Explain how the customer will discover and benefit from it, applying the selected mode's bar. Do not invent a feature distinction to make replication acceptable in livelihood mode.

If changing the business model, identify who pays, why they pay, delivery costs and effects on customer trust. A free user and a paying sponsor are different customers with different evidence requirements. Avoid importing the video's illustrative niche ideas as validated opportunities.

### Preserve the product being tested

Record the intended customer outcome, connected capabilities, supportable claims and what the test will actually implement before writing the page. Distinguish five decisions: the product proposition, the initial audience, the marketing angle, the acquisition channel and the test artifact. One initial channel or one clear headline does not mean one feature. A complete coherent offer can be shown by a small marketing MVP without building the full product.

Use the retained project's full product reference as the shared scope record. Keep a capability-to-artifact coverage table there, identifying clear demonstrations, partial or buried mentions and missing areas. Check the actual page before declaring whole-offer readiness. Preserve the distinction between accepted scope, fictional examples and implemented product behavior; do not create competing definitions in each handoff.

Do not silently replace the user's idea with a smaller product to satisfy a generic focus rule. If a subset is useful, identify the reason, the changed question and what it cannot establish about the original offer. Preserve explicit user scope and resolve a material mismatch before treating the subset as the agreed experiment. Routine implementation choices within that scope do not require a new approval gate.

Use different pain-point messages to learn which needs attract relevant people when that is the question. Keep them connected to the intended offer. Customer research should permit needs and desired solutions outside the assistant's favorite angle, while remaining brief and collecting only necessary information. A clear umbrella promise is compatible with multiple supporting capabilities; an indiscriminate list of unrelated benefits is not required.

### Show the offer before requiring a conversation

Do not turn customer research into a mandatory interview-first sequence. When discovery and low-friction consumer acquisition are the question, use the video's marketing-MVP pattern: relevant traffic -> a concrete landing-page offer or preview -> a clear call to action -> short voluntary questions -> opt-in launch notification. Optional conversations can follow with interested respondents who agree to them; a booked call is not the primary smoke-test conversion. Visitors can discover both the problem and a possible solution through the demonstration. An interactive prototype is optional, not a substitute prerequisite for a landing-page smoke test.

Make availability clear on the page and call to action. A professional presentation of a proposed product does not require implying an unavailable product is live. Early clicks, completed surveys and signups can justify a bounded next experiment without proving payment or retention. Do not require hypothetical willingness-to-pay statements or a universal pre-sale count before creating the test assets. Show a price if price-qualified interest is the hypothesis, and distinguish acceptance of a displayed price from a purchase. Diagnose weak reach separately from offer rejection. Keep interviews available as a research method for either B2C or B2B, chosen for the question and the participant's motivation.

At the transition from the offer to the quiz, explain availability and why the questions help shape the product. Cover the visitor's relevant context, difficulty, severity, attempted solutions and desired help without presupposing a single preferred problem. Offer an appropriate way to describe an unlisted need. The supplied video discloses the unavailable app after the click; this OS also makes pre-launch status clear on the page. Label that as an intentional adaptation. Learning from responses is a purpose of the test alongside opt-in counts.

## 4. Choose one initial channel

Choose the channel most likely to reach qualified prospects and answer the current question within the founder's time, skills and cash limits. Record the relevant audience location and access evidence. No existing audience is a constraint to design around, not an automatic rejection. Start with one primary channel unless the experiment specifically needs a comparison and has enough exposure for it.

| Candidate | What would justify testing it | Measure beyond attention |
|---|---|---|
| Direct outreach or participation in relevant communities | Identifiable prospects, access and a reason for a useful conversation | Qualified replies, conversations held, commitments and purchases; include sourcing and follow-up time |
| Short-form content | Relevant people use the platform and the founder can repeatedly demonstrate the problem or outcome | Qualified visits and downstream actions by topic; views alone do not establish demand |
| Long-form content or SEO | Evidence of relevant questions/search intent, useful material to publish and time to learn | Relevant visits, offer actions and customer conversion; allow for delayed discovery and changing search conditions |
| Paid advertising | A specific audience/intent hypothesis, suitable offer and a bounded affordable learning budget | Qualified visits, offer actions and paid conversion by message; clicks and cheap leads are not customer acquisition |
| Partners, existing networks or referrals | A concrete source of trusted access and a reason for that party to participate | Introductions delivered, adoption and payments; separate hypothetical reach from access secured |

Outbound means initiating contact; inbound means customers finding and approaching the offer. These are useful descriptions, not a B2B/B2C rule. Paid ads are a distribution option, not a guarantee of clean causal evidence. Automated delivery, audience selection and small samples can distort comparisons. Organic channels also cost founder time. Do not prescribe the same channel for every product.

## 5. Write the experiment before running it

Use this schema in the project's existing output location, or `outputs/[YYYY-MM-DD]-[idea-slug]/demand-test.md` when no appropriate record exists:

1. **Decision and hypothesis:** the one uncertainty, why it matters and which result would change the next investment.
2. **Audience:** inclusion/exclusion criteria, geography, actual current behavior/awareness, recruitment source and how exposure will be counted.
3. **Offer and action:** intended product outcome and connected capabilities, customer-language message, supportable claims, availability, price shown if testing payment, and the specific next action. Distinguish the marketed proposition from the implemented artifact and any message variants. Explain whether this is an interview, demo, waitlist, pilot or purchase test.
4. **Channel and assets:** one initial channel, recruitment approach, copy/page/demo requirements and the path from exposure to action. A landing page is optional. A conversation, demonstration or manual service can answer some questions more cheaply.
5. **Bounds and economics:** timebox, cash cap, founder-hour cap, relevant approval already held, expected delivery/support burden and assumptions. Keep projected economics separate from measured results.
6. **Measurement and decision rules:** primary behavior, numerator and denominator, qualified-exposure target, follow-up window, response patterns to learn from, and thresholds for proceeding, revising or stopping. Explain how these thresholds relate to the decision and identify their origin; do not use a universal waitlist size or ad budget. Include an inconclusive outcome when exposure, quality or measurement is inadequate. Set rules before seeing results and revisit their applicability when the offer changes.
7. **Result and next move:** initially mark this as planned. After execution, record dates, actual exposure and audience mix, counts, spend, hours, objections, outcomes and limits. State what changed in the hypothesis and the next bounded action.

Use an honest pre-launch or pilot description. Explain availability before requesting a commitment; do not present an unavailable product as ready to use. Collect only the information needed for the test and distinguish permission for a research conversation from permission for marketing. If payment is part of a future test, its delivery terms must be concrete. A proposed test or budget is not authorization to publish, contact people or spend. Reuse existing explicit authorization where it covers the action; do not add repeat approval steps.

Agent 2 implements the requested test assets here by default after recording its design/build decisions. Apply `memory/build-rules.md` directly. If the user chooses another execution environment, export a self-contained brief containing the full current build-rules file under "STANDING BUILD RULES (implementation must follow)." Keep the asset scoped to the experiment. Agent 3 checks that the offer, source attribution and call to action match the experiment; Agent 2 checks the actual user path, failure states and data handling before claiming readiness.

## 6. Interpret the evidence at its actual strength

Keep exposure, qualified visits, clicks, signups, conversations held, paid commitments, delivered value, active use and retention separate. A waitlist indicates stated interest in an offer; a purchase supports a stronger payment claim; continued useful use and renewal answer different questions again. None proves every later step. Deduplicate people, show counts alongside rates, and identify incentivized, friendly or unrepresentative samples. Do not extrapolate category awareness from a self-selected group of people who already clicked an ad.

Diagnose where the path failed before changing the product: failure to reach relevant people, unclear message, weak offer, price objection, onboarding friction or failure to deliver value require different responses. Few signups after little qualified exposure is inconclusive. Low-cost clicks without downstream action do not justify scaling. Do not select only high pain-score respondents and describe them as representative of the market.

For economic claims, distinguish cost per lead from cost per acquired paying customer. Include founder labor and delivery/support costs when assessing viability. Compare acquisition cost with a conservative contribution over a stated period; do not invent lifetime value from unobserved retention. Identify repeat-use frequency and churn risks appropriate to the customer's job.

## 7. Carry learning into the smallest useful product

If the next justified step is a product build, Agent 2 receives the evidence, remaining assumptions and one essential path from arrival to a useful customer outcome. Scope the MVP to deliver that outcome and test the remaining risk, with a time/budget bound and explicit deferrals. A technical feasibility spike may be needed first. Fast coding does not establish demand, operational readiness or a universal two-day build estimate.

Measure activation as delivered customer value, then payment and continued use over a suitable interval. A smoke-test signup is not product activation. Agent 3 carries its tested message, audience, channel, economics and failures into later launch work; it should not restart marketing from a blank page. Agent 2 carries the reviewed design and technical learning into the next build. Preserve authorization across these handoffs, and do not infer a full-product go from experiment permission.

## 8. Write back without inventing progress

For a retained project, save the experiment and results in its output location; maintain concise decisions, current state and unresolved questions. Update the index only for a meaningful status or verdict change. An unpassed experiment uses its existing output record when the user explicitly chooses to retain it. Otherwise apply the count-only rejected-idea retention rule in principles section 7. Do not create a PASS folder for a test. Supportive, adverse and inconclusive experiment outcomes are evidence descriptions, not additional Agent 1 verdicts.

No automatic PASS, full-build approval or `gtm-planned` stage follows from writing a test or collecting signups. Report precisely what was planned, executed and learned.
